# 2.AA Sex-Stratified Differential Methylation Analysis with Surrogate Variable Adjustment

## 2.AA.1 Rationale

The sex-stratified models fitted in the preceding stage (Section 2.Z) were found not to calibrate cleanly: bacon's inflation estimate remained elevated in both the female stratum (lambda = 2.285) and the male stratum (lambda = 1.189) even after bias/inflation correction, and a classical genomic-control estimate computed independently of bacon was substantially higher again in both (12.1 and 4.1 respectively), indicating that a material amount of genome-wide test-statistic inflation was not resolved by cell-type, age, and smoking adjustment alone. Because the acquisition strategy adopted for this chapter (Section 2.X.1) uses the GEO-deposited, processed series matrix rather than raw IDAT files, array batch and processing-plate identifiers, a standard source of exactly this kind of residual technical variation, are not recoverable from the data available. Surrogate variable analysis (SVA; Leek & Storey, 2007) was applied as a principled, established alternative: rather than requiring the confounding source to be named and measured directly, SVA estimates latent axes of variation in the methylation data that are independent of the biological variable of interest, so that they can be entered as ordinary covariates in the differential methylation model in place of the unavailable batch identifier.

## 2.AA.2 Data preparation

Input data (`beta_raw.rds`, `pheno_celltype.rds`), the smoking-status exclusion, the cell-type covariate selection, and the beta-to-M-value transformation are identical to those already described in Section 2.Z.2, applied to the same 687-sample, smoking-resolved cohort (491 female, 196 male).

## 2.AA.3 Surrogate variable estimation

Within each sex stratum independently, surrogate variables were estimated with `sva::sva()`, contrasting a full model (`~ group + age + smoking + ` cell-type covariates) against the same model with the `group` term removed. This full-versus-null contrast is what allows SVA to estimate latent structure while protecting variation attributable to the RA/Control comparison itself from being absorbed into the surrogate variables (Leek & Storey, 2007). Estimation was restricted to the 20,000 most variable probes within each stratum, the same top-variable-probe convention already used for quality-control principal component analysis in Section 2.X.5, using the iteratively reweighted ("irw") estimation method with five refinement iterations (package defaults). This procedure is computationally intensive at the scale of this cohort and was run once per stratum rather than repeated or tuned against the resulting significance counts, so that the number of surrogate variables retained reflects the method's own internal stopping criterion rather than a choice made to achieve a particular downstream result.

## 2.AA.4 Sex-stratified models with surrogate-variable adjustment

The estimated surrogate variables were appended as additional covariates to the full model matrix, and a linear model was fitted to the M-value matrix with `limma::lmFit()` followed by empirical Bayes moderation (`limma::eBayes()`), exactly as in Section 2.Z.3. The moderated t-statistic for the `group` coefficient was extracted for every retained probe and passed to `bacon::bacon()` (van Iterson, van Zwet & BIOS Consortium, 2017) for bias/inflation correction ahead of Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction, applied independently within each stratum. Each CpG's descriptive effect size (Δbeta, computed on the beta scale, RA minus Control) was retained alongside the corrected statistics, unchanged in definition from Section 2.Z.3.

## 2.AA.5 Gene panel construction

As in Section 2.Z.4, each sex stratum's own bacon-corrected, genome-wide Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-significant (FDR < 0.05) CpGs constitute that sex's panel directly, mapped to an annotated gene via `ChAMPdata::probe.features$gene` and classified as hyper- or hypomethylated in RA relative to Control by the sign of Δbeta.

## 2.AA.6 Male suggestive panel

The male stratum, at n=196, is markedly smaller than the female stratum (n=491), and retained no CpG at genome-wide FDR-significance after surrogate-variable correction (Section 2.AA.8). To still characterise which CpGs come closest to a male-specific association, a second, explicitly non-significant candidate set was constructed for the male stratum only: CpGs with a bacon-corrected p-value below a fixed nominal threshold of 1x10^-4, without Benjamini-Hochberg correction for the 412,492 tests performed. This threshold is deliberately not adjusted for genome-wide multiple testing, and the resulting list is reported as a hypothesis-generating, exploratory candidate set, not as a claim of statistical significance; it is kept structurally and namewise distinct from the female panel and from the male stratum's own (empty) genome-wide-significant panel, so the two categories of evidence cannot be conflated when read from the exported tables alone.

## 2.AA.7 Residuals for downstream co-methylation network analysis

The male stratum's absence of any genome-wide-significant CpG (Section 2.AA.6) reflects a power limitation at the level of single-CpG testing (Section 2.AA.8), not an absence of usable signal for every subsequent method: a planned later stage of this thesis applies weighted co-methylation network analysis (WGCNA) to identify modules of correlated CpGs and test whether module-level summaries, rather than individual CpGs, associate with RA status. That method requires its own, differently prepared input, and it was prepared here, alongside the DMP analysis, so that it is available when needed.

For each sex stratum, the same 20,000 top-variable probes already selected for surrogate-variable estimation (Section 2.AA.3) were residualized against the null model used in that same step, namely age, smoking status, the cell-type covariates, and the stratum's own estimated surrogate variables, fitted via `limma::lmFit()`, with residuals obtained as the difference between the observed and fitted M-values under that model. The `group` (RA/Control) term was deliberately excluded from this residualizing model: including it would remove exactly the RA-associated variation that a subsequent module-trait correlation step is intended to detect, silently guaranteeing a null result at that later stage regardless of the true underlying biology. The resulting residual matrices, one per sex, retain co-methylation structure not already explained by known and latent technical covariates, while preserving any RA-associated signal, and are retained as the input for that later network analysis; no network construction, module detection, or module-trait correlation was performed in this script.

## 2.AA.8 Results

**Cohort.** Of the 687-sample, smoking-resolved cohort (Section 2.AA.2), 491 samples were female (238 Control, 253 RA) and 196 were male (95 Control, 101 RA); both sex-stratified models in this section were fitted on these respective sample sets.

**Calibration.** Surrogate variables were introduced into the sex-stratified models specifically because the corresponding models in Section 2.Z, adjusted only for age, smoking, and cell-type composition, did not calibrate cleanly: bacon's own inflation estimate remained elevated in both strata, and a classical genomic-control estimate computed independently of bacon was substantially higher again in both, indicating a residual source of genome-wide test-statistic inflation that those covariates alone did not capture, and that array batch or plate identifiers, the usual explanation for exactly this pattern, could not be obtained from the processed series-matrix data used throughout this chapter (Section 2.X.1). Adding surrogate variables, estimated without reference to `group`, materially improved calibration in both strata (Table 2.AA.1). SVA identified 83 surrogate variables in the female stratum and 40 in the male stratum. Female-stratum bacon inflation fell from 2.285 (Section 2.Z.6) to 1.200; male-stratum bacon inflation fell more modestly, from 1.189 to 1.151, consistent with the male stratum's original inflation already having been closer to the ideal value of 1.

**Female panel.** Eighteen CpGs reached genome-wide significance in the female stratum after surrogate-variable adjustment, mapping to fourteen annotated genes (three CpGs were intergenic; one gene, *NLRC5*, was represented by two independent CpGs, both hypomethylated in RA). Of the eighteen CpGs, twelve were hypermethylated in RA relative to Control and six were hypomethylated. The most significant association (cg16411857, *NLRC5*, FDR = 2.7x10^-4) was hypomethylated in RA; the largest effect size in either direction (cg21459645, *CYP27A1*, Δbeta = +0.050, FDR = 6.5x10^-3) was hypermethylated. Among the fourteen annotated genes, *IL6R* is notable given its established role as a therapeutic target in RA, though no claim of mechanistic relevance beyond the statistical association reported here is made.

**Male panel.** No CpG reached genome-wide significance in the male stratum after surrogate-variable adjustment. Unlike the corresponding result in Section 2.Z.6, this null result is interpreted as a properly calibrated finding rather than an inflation artefact: the nominal (uncorrected) bacon p<0.05 rate across all tested CpGs fell to 5.6% after surrogate-variable adjustment, closely matching the 5% expected under a correctly calibrated null distribution (against 19.6% before adjustment), indicating the male-stratum model is no longer substantially inflated. The absence of any genome-wide-significant CpG at n=196 is therefore most plausibly attributable to limited statistical power in the smaller male stratum rather than to unresolved technical confounding.

**Male suggestive panel.** At the nominal bacon-corrected p<1x10^-4 threshold defined in Section 2.AA.6, 70 CpGs were retained, mapping to 56 annotated genes. Under a correctly calibrated null this threshold would be expected to retain approximately 41 CpGs by chance alone (412,492 tests x 1x10^-4); the observed count of 70 is a modest excess over that chance expectation, consistent with this list containing a mixture of true, individually weak associations and false positives, rather than being either entirely noise or a set of confidently established findings. No gene in this list should be treated as validated without independent replication or a substantially larger male-stratum sample.

**Residuals for downstream network analysis.** The procedure described in Section 2.AA.7 produced a 20,000 x 491 residual matrix for the female stratum and a 20,000 x 196 residual matrix for the male stratum, one row per top-variable probe and one column per sample in that stratum. No further analysis of these matrices (network construction, module detection, or module-trait correlation) was performed at this stage.

**Relevance of region-level analysis to the planned mQTL stage.** The single-CpG results reported above motivate, rather than substitute for, a subsequent differentially methylated region (DMR) analysis, built on the full, surrogate-variable-adjusted per-CpG statistics produced by this stage rather than on either gene panel in isolation. The surrogate-variable-adjusted statistics are specified here deliberately, rather than the unadjusted equivalent produced in Section 2.Z: because a DMR method aggregates neighbouring CpGs' statistics into a single regional summary, any residual technical inflation present in those underlying statistics does not cancel out through aggregation and can instead be amplified into a large number of spuriously "significant" regions. This is not a hypothetical concern: an exploratory DMR analysis built on the unadjusted Section 2.Z statistics (not otherwise reported in this chapter) returned the great majority of its candidate regions as nominally significant in both sexes, an implausible yield consistent with exactly this failure mode, and confirming that DMR calling in this cohort requires the calibrated, surrogate-variable-adjusted statistics produced here rather than the uncorrected ones.

Concretely, this means all 412,492 CpGs tested in this stage are carried forward into the DMR stage for each sex, in both the female and the male stratum: the FDR<0.05 filter that defines the panels in Sections 2.AA.5-2.AA.6 (eighteen CpGs in females, zero in males) is not applied before DMR calling, since a CpG's individual significance status is not what a DMR method conditions its region-building on. Where FDR<0.05 filtering is relevant is downstream of DMR calling, applied instead to the resulting region-level statistic, at the level of regions rather than of the individual CpGs that entered the analysis. DMR methods aggregate spatially adjacent, correlated CpGs into a single region-level statistic, which both reduces the effective multiple-testing burden relative to 412,492 independent single-CpG tests and gains power specifically when the true effect at a locus is distributed across several neighbouring CpGs rather than concentrated at one, a pattern this chapter has reason to expect given the diffuse, low-magnitude signal already characterised in Section 2.Z.5. This is expected to matter most for the male stratum, where single-CpG testing was well calibrated (Section 2.AA.8) but underpowered at n=196: a coordinated shift across several correlated CpGs within a region can reach region-level significance even where none of its constituent CpGs individually does.

Region-level, rather than single-CpG, output is also the more natural unit for the Mendelian randomisation and colocalisation stage this chapter is working towards. That stage requires identifying, for each candidate locus, an external methylation quantitative trait locus (mQTL), a SNP associated with methylation at that locus, to serve as the genetic instrument; published mQTL resources typically characterise genetic influence on methylation across a local region of correlated CpGs rather than at a single, isolated probe, and DMRs are conventionally annotated directly to their overlapping gene, providing a natural region-to-gene correspondence for querying such resources. A gene supported by a DMR, several concordant, spatially adjacent CpGs rather than one significant probe in isolation, is accordingly a more robust and more directly usable candidate for mQTL lookup and subsequent colocalisation than a single-CpG hit, particularly for the male stratum, where no single CpG reached genome-wide significance at all.
