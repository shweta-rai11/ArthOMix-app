# 2.FF Mendelian Randomisation of the Majority-Vote CpG Panel Against Rheumatoid Arthritis

## 2.FF.1 Rationale

The ensemble feature selection described in Section 2.EE produced, for each sex stratum, a majority-vote panel of CpGs supported by at least two of three independent, differently biased machine-learning methods (female stratum, 12 CpGs; male stratum, 9 CpGs; Section 2.EE.3). Every analysis upstream of this section -- differentially methylated position (DMP) testing, differentially methylated region (DMR) calling, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) module membership, and the ensemble machine-learning panel itself -- establishes an *association* between methylation at a given CpG and rheumatoid arthritis (RA) case-control status, adjusted for measured confounders such as age, smoking, and estimated cell-type composition. None of these designs can rule out reverse causation, whereby disease-driven changes in blood cell composition or systemic inflammatory state alter methylation, nor can they rule out unmeasured confounding. Mendelian randomisation (MR) is applied here to pose a sharper question of the same candidate panel: whether methylation at each of these CpGs is *causally* upstream of RA risk, using genetic variants -- cis-acting methylation quantitative trait loci (cis-mQTLs) -- as instruments. Because such variants are fixed at conception, they are not susceptible to the reverse causation or lifestyle confounding that limits the observational DMP, DMR, and WGCNA associations.

**Exposure and outcome definition.** The exposure is DNA methylation at each candidate CpG, instrumented by that CpG's cis-mQTLs drawn from the Genetics of DNA Methylation Consortium (GoDMC; Min et al., 2021). The outcome is rheumatoid arthritis case-control status, drawn from the European-ancestry subset of Ishigaki et al. (2022; GWAS Catalog accession GCST90132223). The analysis follows a forward-direction design only, testing methylation as a cause of RA risk. A reverse design, in which RA genetic liability instruments methylation as the outcome, was considered but not implemented for this panel: the panel was constructed specifically to test whether these observationally associated CpGs are plausibly upstream of disease, rather than to characterise disease-driven methylation change. Exposure-outcome identity was verified programmatically at multiple stages of the analysis pipeline to guard against an inadvertently reversed or mislabelled assignment.

**Choice of outcome dataset.** Ishigaki et al. (2022) was selected as the outcome GWAS in preference to the earlier RA genetics consortium release of Okada et al. (2014), of which it is the direct successor. Ishigaki et al.'s European-ancestry subset (22,350 cases, 74,823 controls; 97,173 total) is both larger than the comparable European subset available from Okada et al. and more closely ancestry-matched to GoDMC's own European-ancestry mQTL reference panel (~27,750 samples). Ancestry mismatch between exposure and outcome GWAS is itself a recognised source of bias in MR, arising from differential linkage-disequilibrium structure between populations, so this substitution represents a genuine methodological improvement rather than a change in sample size alone. Ishigaki et al. (2022) does not have a confirmed entry in the OpenGWAS/IEU database; the outcome summary statistics were therefore obtained directly from the GWAS Catalog's own hosted repository, using the non-harmonised GRCh37/hg19 build to match the genome build used by GoDMC and throughout this chapter. Correct parsing of this file was independently confirmed by recovery of the well-established RA risk variant rs2476601 (*PTPN22*) at genome-wide significance, providing an external check beyond mere absence of a runtime error.

**Rationale for an mQTL-instrumented design over a genome-wide eQTL-MR template.** A natural methodological template for a screen of this kind is genome-wide expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) MR, in which instruments for thousands of genes are tested uniformly with a standard multi-method suite (inverse-variance weighted, MR-Egger, weighted median, MR-PRESSO) and narrowed by convergence with independent differential-expression and network evidence (e.g. Wang et al., 2025) -- structurally the same multi-omics-convergence logic used throughout this chapter's Sections 2.CC-2.EE. Two features of a CpG-level, mQTL-instrumented design do not transfer directly from that template and are addressed explicitly rather than inherited by default. First, instrument sparsity: eQTL genes routinely carry dozens of independent cis-eQTLs, whereas a single CpG typically yields only one to three independent cis-mQTLs after linkage-disequilibrium (LD) clumping, below which MR-Egger, Cochran's Q, and MR-PRESSO are not meaningfully estimable. The applied estimator set is therefore tiered by each CpG's own instrument count (Section 2.FF.2) rather than applied uniformly and reported as undefined for the majority of CpGs. Second, the outcome GWAS used here, like its predecessor, is not sex-stratified; although the candidate panel itself was constructed from sex-stratified evidence throughout this chapter, this MR step necessarily tests whether a CpG's methylation causally affects RA risk in the general population rather than specifically in the sex from which it was identified, a scope limitation carried forward to Section 2.FF.4.

**Addressing LD/haplotype pleiotropy through colocalization.** A cis-mQTL variant can simultaneously associate with the expression of a nearby gene and with several neighbouring CpGs on the same haplotype, a form of pleiotropy that MR-Egger and Cochran's Q cannot detect when instrument counts are low. Bayesian colocalization was therefore run per CpG, comparing GoDMC's cis-region association profile against the RA GWAS's regional association profile, as the intended complement specifically for CpGs with too few instruments to support a formal pleiotropy test. Unlike MR-Egger and Cochran's Q, colocalization does not require multiple genome-wide-significant instruments to be informative, only sufficient shared variant coverage across the region in both datasets. The resolution of this check on the GoDMC side is nonetheless bounded by how that resource was constructed: it comprises a candidate list thresholded at a lenient significance level in each contributing cohort prior to meta-analysis, rather than an exhaustive scan of every variant against every CpG -- denser than the clumped MR instrument set, but not equivalent to an exhaustive per-variant test.

## 2.FF.2 Methods

**Candidate panel.** The candidate set comprised the union of the female (n = 12) and male (n = 9) majority-vote CpG panels derived from ensemble feature selection (Section 2.EE.3), giving 21 CpGs in total. The two sex-specific panels were confirmed to be disjoint, so no CpG was tested under more than one sex-derived provenance.

**CpG genomic position.** Chromosome and base-pair coordinates (hg19/GRCh37, matching both the mQTL and outcome GWAS builds used below) for each candidate CpG were obtained from the Illumina manifest positions provided by the ChAMPdata Bioconductor annotation package.

**Instrument source and selection.** Genetic instruments were derived from cis-mQTL summary statistics from the GoDMC consortium (Min et al., 2021), the largest publicly available meta-analysis of genetic effects on blood DNA methylation (~27,750 European-ancestry samples). GoDMC's own variant identifiers are not dbSNP reference SNP IDs; these were resolved to rsids via a paired lookup file distributed by the same resource, and any variant that could not be resolved to a true rsid (untyped indels) was excluded and counted. The GoDMC association resource used here is a pre-filtered candidate list -- each contributing cohort's own scan was thresholded at a lenient p < 1x10^-5 before meta-analysis, rather than reporting an exhaustive per-SNP test against every CpG -- a property of the source data that is carried forward into the colocalization analysis below and discussed further in Section 2.FF.4.

Instruments were restricted to associations flagged as cis (variant within 1 Mb of the CpG) at genome-wide significance (P < 5x10^-8), a threshold at least as stringent as GoDMC's own public cis-significance criterion (P < 1x10^-8). Each variant's cis status was independently cross-checked against this project's own CpG coordinates rather than relying on the source annotation alone. Surviving variants were then LD-clumped per CpG (r^2 < 0.001, 10,000 kb window) using the clumping procedure implemented in the ieugwasr R package (Hemani et al., 2018) and retained only if instrument strength reached F >= 10, the conventional threshold for adequate instrument strength in two-sample MR.

**Outcome.** Rheumatoid arthritis case-control status was taken from the European-ancestry subset of Ishigaki et al. (2022) (GWAS Catalog accession GCST90132223; 22,350 cases, 74,823 controls), read from the non-harmonised GRCh37 release of the summary statistics using the outcome-import procedure of the TwoSampleMR R package (Hemani et al., 2018) (see Section 2.FF.1 for the rationale for this dataset over Okada et al., 2014). This release provides no per-variant effect-allele frequency or sample-size field; study-level totals were substituted for the missing per-SNP sample size, while the absent allele frequency meant that palindromic instrument SNPs could not be strand-resolved and were excluded during harmonisation rather than retained under an unverified assumption.

**Harmonisation.** Exposure and outcome summary statistics were harmonised using the TwoSampleMR R package's harmonisation procedure (Hemani et al., 2018), under the setting that drops ambiguous palindromic variants rather than retaining them: palindromic variants were resolved by allele frequency where possible and dropped otherwise, and rows failing the harmonisation quality flag were removed. Exposure and outcome identity were asserted programmatically at three stages of the pipeline (post-clumping, post-outcome-extraction, post-harmonisation) to guard against a mislabelled or reversed exposure-outcome assignment.

**Steiger filtering.** The harmonised instrument set was passed through directionality (Steiger) filtering (Hemani, Tilling & Davey Smith, 2017) to flag any instrument for which the outcome, rather than the exposure, explains a greater share of phenotypic variance -- an instrument-level check against reverse causation, complementary to the forward-only design adopted at the panel level (Section 2.FF.1).

**Tiered MR estimation.** Because cis-mQTL instrument counts are typically sparse at the level of a single CpG, the set of MR estimators applied was tiered by each CpG's own clumped instrument count rather than applied uniformly across the panel:

| Instruments | Method(s) applied |
|---|---|
| 1 | Wald ratio only |
| 2 | Inverse-variance weighted (IVW; Burgess, Butterworth & Thompson, 2013) only -- MR-Egger and weighted median are not meaningfully estimable with two instruments |
| >=3 | IVW (primary) + MR-Egger (Bowden, Davey Smith & Burgess, 2015) + weighted median (Bowden et al., 2016) + weighted mode and simple mode (Hartwig, Davey Smith & Bowden, 2017) |

**Sensitivity analyses.** For CpGs reaching the >=3-instrument tier, the MR-Egger intercept test (directional pleiotropy), Cochran's Q statistic (heterogeneity, computed for both IVW and MR-Egger), and leave-one-out re-estimation were additionally computed. CpGs below this tier carry no such pleiotropy check, a limitation reported alongside their point estimates rather than left implicit.

**Reporting.** Per-CpG forest plots of single-instrument and combined MR estimates were generated using the TwoSampleMR R package (Hemani et al., 2018), rendered at this thesis's fixed figure canvas size with no in-plot title, consistent with the figure convention used throughout.

**Colocalization.** For every CpG carried forward into MR, Bayesian colocalization analysis (Giambartolomei et al., 2014), implemented in the coloc R package, was performed between the GoDMC cis-mQTL association profile and the RA GWAS association profile across the same +/-1 Mb window. The GoDMC side of this comparison used the same candidate-list rows and rsid resolution described above, with no additional significance threshold applied on top of GoDMC's own candidate-list filtering; the RA side used the full, dense set of variants available in the same Ishigaki et al. (2022) summary statistics used for outcome extraction, without a significance filter, providing denser regional coverage on the outcome side than on the exposure side. The outcome case fraction was set from the confirmed study-level case and control totals; effect-allele frequency was not required, and was therefore omitted, because it is only needed when approximating Bayes factors from p-values rather than from directly supplied effect estimates and standard errors. A CpG-locus pair was classified as coloc-supported when the posterior probability of a single shared causal variant (PP.H4) reached >= 0.8, coloc-refuted when the posterior probability of two distinct, LD-linked causal variants (PP.H3) reached >= 0.8, and inconclusive otherwise.

**Analysis structure.** Instrument extraction, outcome extraction, and harmonisation were performed once for the combined 21-CpG panel, since the mQTL source and outcome GWAS are identical regardless of sex. MR estimation was then run independently on each sex's own subset of the harmonised data, mirroring the self-contained, per-sex analysis structure used throughout this chapter (e.g. Section 2.EE). Colocalization was performed independently of, and after, sex-specific MR estimation, reusing the CpG positions and outcome metadata resolved during instrument preparation.

## 2.FF.3 Results

**Instrument availability, rather than the MR estimation itself, was the dominant constraint on this analysis.** Of the 21 candidate CpGs (12 female-panel, 9 male-panel), 9 had no GoDMC candidate association at all, not even at GoDMC's lenient per-study screening threshold of p < 1x10^-5 used to construct the candidate list (Section 2.FF.2), let alone at genome-wide significance. This absence of any candidate row was independently re-confirmed for one of these CpGs by a targeted search directly against the raw source file, ruling out a pipeline artefact. Notably, this set includes cg21740204 (*SMC5*), the female stratum's strict three-way machine-learning consensus CpG (Section 2.EE.3) and the single CpG with the strongest convergent observational evidence in this chapter (differential methylation, WGCNA module membership, differentially methylated region, and support from all three ensemble machine-learning methods). In GoDMC's ~27,750-sample European blood reference, this CpG shows no detectable local or distal genetic influence on its own methylation -- an informative null result in its own right, since not every disease-associated CpG has an underlying genetic (mQTL) architecture, and MR is mechanically impossible in its absence.

A further 4 CpGs had some GoDMC candidate rows but none reaching this analysis's cis-significance threshold (P < 5x10^-8, restricted to variants flagged cis and independently cross-checked against the project's own CpG coordinates). After LD clumping (r^2 < 0.001, 10,000 kb window) and instrument-strength filtering (F >= 10), 8 of the 21 candidate CpGs carried usable instruments into MR: 6 of the female panel's 12 and 2 of the male panel's 9. All 8 fell into the sparsest instrument tiers described in Section 2.FF.2 (one instrument for 6 CpGs, two instruments for 2 CpGs); none reached the three-instrument threshold required for MR-Egger, Cochran's Q, or leave-one-out analysis, so that sensitivity suite is not reported for any CpG in this screen. Steiger filtering flagged no instrument as outcome-driven across all ten SNP-CpG pairs tested, i.e. every instrument explained more variance in methylation than in RA case status, consistent with the forward-direction design adopted for this analysis.

No CpG, in either sex, reached nominal significance (all p >= 0.27). Given the instrument-count ceiling documented above, this null result should be read as a genuine absence of detected effect at the statistical power achieved -- a single- or two-instrument Wald-ratio/IVW estimate has wide confidence intervals by construction -- rather than as strong positive evidence against a causal effect.

**Colocalization analysis independently converged on the same conclusion.** Seven of the eight CpGs carried into MR produced a colocalization result; the region surrounding cg23881902 contained fewer than ten GoDMC variants even in the unfiltered candidate-list pull, so colocalization could not be computed for it. For every one of the remaining seven CpGs, the posterior probability of association with methylation alone, and no association with RA in the same region (PP.H1), dominated (range 0.94-0.99). At every locus tested, there was essentially no detectable RA genetic signal available to colocalize with, independent of the strength of the methylation association itself. No CpG reached the coloc-supported threshold (PP.H4 >= 0.8) or the coloc-refuted threshold (PP.H3 >= 0.8); all seven were formally inconclusive by that criterion, but the consistently PP.H1-dominant pattern is itself a coherent result, congruent with the non-significant MR estimates above: two independent methods -- sparse-instrument MR and regional colocalization -- converge on an absence of detectable RA genetic association at these specific loci, not merely an absence of a methylation-mediated one.

**Taken together**, of the 21 CpGs entering this MR screen with the strongest observational evidence (differential methylation, differentially methylated regions, WGCNA module membership, and ensemble machine-learning support) in this chapter, the majority (13/21) had no genetic instrument available in GoDMC at all, and the remaining 8 showed no evidence, from either MR or colocalization, of a causal effect on RA risk at the achieved statistical power. This does not overturn the observational associations documented in Sections 2.BB-2.EE, which remain valid associational findings; it indicates that the data available to this analysis cannot currently support upgrading any of them to a causal claim.

## 2.FF.4 Limitations

**Sex-combined outcome.** No adequately powered, publicly available sex-stratified RA GWAS summary statistics exist as an alternative to Ishigaki et al. (2022) or its predecessor Okada et al. (2014): RA's approximately 3:1 female-to-male case ratio means a male-only GWAS stratum would be severely underpowered even where one had been attempted, and the single sex-specific RA GWAS analysis identified through a literature search was small in scale and was not deposited as full genome-wide summary statistics usable here. Ishigaki et al. (2022) therefore remains the outcome dataset of necessity rather than convenience. As a result, this MR step establishes whether a CpG's methylation is causally linked to RA in the general population, not specifically in the sex stratum from which that CpG was originally identified (Section 2.FF.1) -- a genuine scope limitation on what these results can claim, and one that is carried forward into the chapter's overall interpretation.

**Missing per-variant allele frequency and sample size in the outcome data.** The outcome summary statistics report no effect-allele-frequency or per-variant sample-size field. Study-level totals were substituted for the missing per-variant sample size, a standard and low-impact simplification, but the missing allele frequency is not similarly recoverable: any candidate instrument that happens to be palindromic (A/T or G/C alleles) cannot be strand-resolved against this outcome and is necessarily excluded during harmonisation rather than retained. For a CpG whose only surviving cis-mQTL instrument is palindromic, this attrition alone is sufficient to leave zero usable instruments for that CpG, independent of any other filtering step.

**Sparse instruments, partially compensated by colocalization.** Most CpGs in both majority-vote panels are expected to yield only one to three independent cis-mQTLs, for which only a Wald-ratio or two-instrument IVW estimate is available, with no accompanying MR-Egger or Cochran's Q pleiotropy check (Section 2.FF.2). Colocalization does not carry the same instrument-count requirement and therefore provides an independent, pleiotropy-relevant signal for exactly these sparse-instrument CpGs. However, a coloc-supported verdict does not retroactively grant a single-instrument MR estimate the same evidential weight as a three-or-more-instrument estimate that has passed a clean Egger-intercept and Cochran's Q check; the two forms of evidence should be interpreted jointly per CpG rather than treated as interchangeable.

**LD/haplotype pleiotropy addressed by colocalization, itself bounded by the construction of the mQTL reference.** Colocalization implements the complementary check for haplotype-level pleiotropy described in Sections 2.FF.1 and 2.FF.2. Because the underlying GoDMC resource is a pre-filtered candidate list, thresholded per contributing cohort prior to meta-analysis rather than an exhaustive scan of every variant, the resolution of the GoDMC side of each colocalization test is correspondingly bounded: denser than the clumped MR instrument set for the same CpG, but not equivalent to a fully exhaustive per-variant regional test. This should be read alongside any posterior-probability estimate reported in Section 2.FF.3 as a known property of the source data rather than a gap specific to this analysis.

**Tissue specificity.** GoDMC mQTLs are derived from whole blood. This is a smaller limitation for RA specifically than it would be for a disease with tissue-restricted pathology: RA's key pathogenic processes -- autoantibody production, T- and B-cell activation, and systemic inflammation -- are substantially blood- and immune-cell-mediated, and blood is the same tissue sampled by this chapter's underlying methylation array data throughout. Blood-derived cis-mQTLs are therefore a defensible choice on biological grounds, not merely a convenient one, though they may still fail to capture regulatory effects specific to synovial tissue, which remains unaddressed by this analysis.
